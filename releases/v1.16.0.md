Round 13 runs Google's Gemini Decision 1, released today, which takes the top Vision Index. Every dataset now has its own leaderboard, and [systemonebench.dev/benchmarks](https://systemonebench.dev/benchmarks/) lists the leader of each one: the 16 text datasets, the 6 image datasets, the System One Index, its English and Arabic sub-indexes, and the Vision Index. No other score changes.

## A page per benchmark
Each page ranks every charted model on that dataset with its accuracy, a 95% interval, macro-F1 (mean IoU for RefCOCO), calibration error and failed calls. It also gives the dataset's source and sample and two baselines, the best constant answer and a random guess, and tags in-domain models. Every dataset chart on the main page links to its page, and each benchmark has a shields.io badge at `badge/bench-<dataset>.json`.

## Leaders, and ties
A model is called a benchmark's leader only when its lead is larger than sampling noise: more than 1.96 standard errors of the difference in accuracy, treating the two runs as independent samples. Otherwise the page says the top score is a statistical tie and names who it's tied with. Every model answers the same cases, so this overstates the noise and errs towards a tie.

With 150 to 200 cases per dataset, most top places are ties: on 19 of the 22 datasets, and on 3 of the 4 indexes, the top score is within sampling noise of at least one other model. The clear leaders are Gemini Decision 1 on the Vision Index (89.2, 3.1 points ahead) and on Oxford-IIIT Pet (98.0%, 5.5 points ahead), Mercury Decide on DAIR Emotion (91.5%, 6.5 points ahead) and Jev 1.14 on RefCOCO, the only model that answers Box questions. Mercury's lead is also flagged as unusually high, below.

## Unusually high scores
A maker's own account only tags the models whose makers publish their training data. So every text score is now also checked against what it should be: a fit of model strength and dataset difficulty (a two-way median polish in log-odds) predicts each score from the model's other datasets and every other model's score on that dataset. A score at least 10 points and 3.5 spreads above its prediction, counting sampling noise, is marked **unusually high** on the leaderboard, the benchmark page and the model page. It often means the model trained on that dataset or one like it. It isn't proof, and the score still counts.

13 scores are flagged, 4 of them on datasets their makers already declared in-domain (Naluri-308M and 279M on DAIR Emotion, Naluri-279M and Dexy-Decision-9B on Arabic MASSIVE scenario). The others:
